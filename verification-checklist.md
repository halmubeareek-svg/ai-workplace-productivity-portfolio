# 🟡 Verification Checklist — Business Consultant

I don't trust AI-generated content, especially numbers and claims, without checking it first. This checklist is based on the four-step Verification Protocol (Identify the content → Set the right criteria → Test it against sources → Confirm, document, and decide).

## My Checklist

- [ ] **Identify the claim** — What exactly is being stated, and where did it come from (my notes, a report, an AI draft)?
- [ ] **Flag high-risk claims** — Does it include a number, a date, a name, or a quote? If yes, treat it as high-risk by default.
- [ ] **Trace it to a trusted source** — Can I point to the specific document, tracker, or person that confirms this?
- [ ] **Confirm exact details** — Do the names, dates, numbers, and figures match the source exactly, not approximately?
- [ ] **Identify uncertainty** — Is there anything in the claim that isn't actually confirmed anywhere? Mark it, don't smooth it over.
- [ ] **Remove unsupported claims** — If something can't be traced or confirmed, take it out rather than leave it in "just in case."
- [ ] **Get human approval before use** — Does a manager, partner, or the client-facing owner sign off before this goes out?

## Applied Example

**Output being checked:** the decision-ready brief from **Section 3 — Information Workflow**, specifically this risk bullet: *"Data-cleanup workstream is trending ~12% over the approved budget."*

I picked this claim, and not the rest of the brief, because it's the one number in that brief — everything else is a date or a request, but this is a figure that could change how seriously the partner treats the risk.

1. **Identify the claim** — A specific, quantified statement that the data-cleanup workstream is ~12% over its approved budget.
2. **Flag as high-risk** — Yes: it's a number, and it will directly shape whether the partner escalates this to the client.
3. **Trace to a trusted source** — Traced back to the original client call notes quoted in Section 3 ("trending about 12% over the approved budget") — not to the AI output alone.
4. **Confirm exact details** — The figure in the brief matches the raw notes exactly; nothing was rounded or exaggerated in the AI's rewrite.
5. **Identify uncertainty** — Flagged: the notes say "trending about 12%," which is an estimate, not a confirmed actuals figure from the finance tracker. This uncertainty was kept visible in the brief rather than smoothed into a hard number.
6. **Remove unsupported claims** — Nothing removed; the claim is supported, provided the "trending/estimate" caveat stays attached to it.
7. **Human approval** — I confirmed the wording with the estimate caveat intact before it was allowed to move from Section 3's brief into anything client-facing.

**Result of verification:** the claim was accurate and traceable to the source notes, but only as an *estimate* — so it was kept in the final brief with the word "trending" preserved, and flagged internally as needing a finance-tracker confirmation before it appears in any client-facing document.
